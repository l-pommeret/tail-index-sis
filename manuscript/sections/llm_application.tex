\section{Application with known nuisance structure: token-surprisal tails
of a language model}\label{sec:llm}

The crime application reads screening ranks without ground truth. This
section builds a second application in which the status of a family of
covariates is known --- in one case exactly --- so that the target
separation demonstrated by model B1 can be observed on real data.

\subsection{Response, data, and regime}

For a document $w_1,\ldots,w_T$ scored by a language model $\theta$, let
$S_t=-\log p_\theta(w_t\mid w_{<t})$ be the token surprisal and take
\[
 Y=\exp\Bigl(\max_{t\le T}S_t\Bigr)
 =\Bigl\{\min_{t\le T}p_\theta(w_t\mid w_{<t})\Bigr\}^{-1}.
\]
If the conditional surprisal tail is exponential,
$\Pp(S>s\mid x)\sim c(x)e^{-s/\gamma(x)}$ --- the behaviour a Zipfian
vocabulary produces --- then
$\Pp(Y>y\mid x)\sim c(x)y^{-1/\gamma(x)}$: conditional regular variation
with $\gamma(x)>0$ holds by the exponential mechanism, and the
subexponential factor becomes the slowly varying factor of (C1). Mean
log-perplexity, by contrast, concentrates and lands in the Gumbel
domain; its marginal can look heavy-tailed by mixture while every
conditional law is light, which would invalidate (C1) silently. The
maximum, not the mean, is the object this framework applies to.

The corpus is a 2025 Common Crawl snapshot (FineWeb), chosen because its
filtering is heuristic: corpora filtered by perplexity or by quality
classifiers truncate the upper tail of $Y$, a selection on the response.
The model is Pythia-410m (fp32 throughout; the reference model of the
likelihood-ratio variant is Pythia-1.4b), whose public training corpus
predates the crawl. The primary analysis scores exactly $T=512$ tokens
per document after a 64-token burn-in, so document length is fixed by
construction; a secondary analysis leaves $T$ free. Covariates are never
computed from the forward pass that produces $Y$: $p=272$ surface and
frozen-encoder-embedding covariates survive a continuity filter, with
$n=20{,}000$ documents, and at the tuning of
Section~\ref{sec:tuning} the regime is $2n\alpha h\approx232$ local
extremes per interior window. Marginal and stratified generalized
Pareto fits give $\gamma\approx1.24$ with profile confidence intervals
excluding $0$ in all sixteen strata examined --- the conditional
positivity that (C1) requires and that a marginal Hill plot cannot
establish. Numerical saturation, degenerate document classes, and
fp32 rounding are ruled out by direct checks in the replication
material.

\subsection{Calibrated screening and a null result}

Ranks alone never refuse to answer: a ranking exists whether or not any
signal does. The screening here is therefore calibrated by permutation:
responses are permuted against covariates, the full screen is rerun, and
the familywise $5\%$ threshold is the appropriate quantile of the
best null score. On the primary analysis, no covariate of the $272$
crosses the familywise threshold, for either response variant; per-covariate
exceedances match their expected counts. The same holds for
$1{,}024$-dimensional blocks of internal model activations (residual-stream
averages over a past prefix, screened against $Y$ computed on the
disjoint future suffix, at $n=50{,}000$): every layer configuration is
at or near chance level. An uncalibrated reading of the raw rankings
would have produced discussable leaders in every one of these
experiments; the calibration is what makes the null legible, and we
regard it as part of the method's proper use.

\subsection{Two scale clusters with known status}

\paragraph{Document length: a natural nuisance, asymptotic truth.}
For weakly dependent regularly varying surprisals,
$\E[\max_{t\le T}S_t\mid x]=\gamma(x)\log T+O(1)$: length enters the
scale of $Y$ and never its index. On nested prefixes of the same
documents --- composition held fixed --- the measured slope of
$\E[\max S]$ against $\log T$ is $1.23$ with intra-document confidence
interval $[1.15,1.31]$, containing the independent Hill estimates of
$\gamma$; across free-length strata the Hill intervals overlap while
the location moves by $2.24$ nats. When $T$ is left free, the length
cluster --- eight correlated proxies from character count to gzip
size --- fills the top five positions of the quantile screen at every
level $\tau\in\{.90,.95,.99\}$ ($5/5$; the chance probability of this
configuration is about $2\times10^{-4}$), while the tail-index screen
places none of them in its top five and ranks the block in its bottom
quartile. This is the B1 dissociation on real data, with a nuisance an
order of magnitude stronger than the simulated one.

The same experiment locates a boundary of validity: at $n=2{,}000$
($2n\alpha h\approx65$) the tail screen itself ranks the length block
first --- the second-order bias of the local Hill statistic responds to
a scale shift of this amplitude --- and the artefact disappears at
$n=20{,}000$ ($2n\alpha h\approx232$). Specificity against scale is an
asymptotic property with a measurable finite-sample onset.

\paragraph{A grafted cluster: exact truth.}
By Breiman's lemma, if $V>0$ is bounded and independent of $Y$, then
$YV$ is regularly varying with the same index, and only the scale
constant changes. Grafting
$V=\exp\{\kappa(\Phi(F)-1/2)\}$ with a latent $F\sim N(0,1)$ and
$q_{\rm scale}=20$ observed proxies of $F$ (loading $0.7$, as in B1)
onto the real responses produces twenty covariates that provably carry
no information about the index while shifting every finite quantile,
inside the real correlation structure of the $272$ genuine covariates.
Across the paired strength sweep $\kappa\in\{0,0.5,1,2,4\}$, the number
of proxies among the twenty leading positions grows for the quantile
screen from $1$ ($\kappa=.5$) to $19$ ($\kappa=2$) and $20$
($\kappa=4$, all three levels; median proxy rank $10.5$ of $292$),
while the tail-index screen retains $2$ to $4$ across the entire sweep
(median proxy rank $143$--$175$, at or beyond the chance value of
$146$). The quantile screen's behaviour is coherent for its own target
throughout; the experiment measures an order-of-magnitude sensitivity
gap, not an immunity --- at $\kappa=4$ the tail screen's contamination
becomes visible ($4$--$5$ proxies, one of them first), and minimum-rank
aggregation does not help here, since there is no dispersed tail signal
to stabilize. Under the permutation calibration, no proxy reaches the
familywise threshold in any arm for any screen: the raw rankings are
more contaminated than the calibrated procedure.

\subsection{What this application does and does not establish}

An external criterion, computed without any screen --- the dispersion of
stratified $\gamma$ estimates per covariate, calibrated against random
strata --- identifies which covariates actually modulate the index in
these data (a single clear case, the whitespace rate, with
non-overlapping quartile intervals). Both screening families rank
$\gamma$-modulating covariates equally well by this criterion, the
quantile screen at $\tau=.95$ marginally better. The evidence of this
section is therefore evidence of \emph{specificity}: covariates that
move finite quantiles without touching the index --- whether by
asymptotic argument (length) or by exact construction (the grafted
cluster) --- capture every fixed-quantile ranking and not the
tail-index ranking. It is not evidence of superior power for finding
index-active covariates, and the simulations of
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} say the same: the advantage of the
tail-index screen is what its leading positions contain, not how often
it finds a shared signal first.
