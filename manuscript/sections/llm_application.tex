\section{Application with known nuisance structure: token-surprisal tails
of a language model}\label{sec:llm}

The crime application reads screening ranks without ground truth. This
section builds a second application in which the status of a family of
covariates is known --- in one case exactly --- so that the target
separation demonstrated by model B1 can be observed on real data.

\subsection{Response, data, and regime}

For a document $w_1,\ldots,w_T$ scored by a language model $\theta$, let
$S_t=-\log p_\theta(w_t\mid w_{<t})$ be the token surprisal and take
\[
 Y=\exp\Bigl(\max_{t\le T}S_t\Bigr)
 =\Bigl\{\min_{t\le T}p_\theta(w_t\mid w_{<t})\Bigr\}^{-1}.
\]
If the conditional surprisal tails are exponential,
$\Pp(S_t>s\mid x)\sim c(x)e^{-s/\gamma(x)}$ --- the behaviour a Zipfian
vocabulary produces --- then each $e^{S_t}$ is conditionally regularly
varying with index $-1/\gamma(x)$, and for weakly dependent surprisals
the maximum inherits the index:
$\Pp(Y>y\mid x)\sim\theta(x)\,T\,c(x)\,y^{-1/\gamma(x)}$, where the
extremal coefficient $\theta(x)$ and $T$ enter the scale only. Weak
dependence of the within-document surprisal series at extreme levels is
an assumption of this application, not a verified fact; the nested-prefix
experiment below supports its first-order consequence. Mean
log-perplexity, by contrast, concentrates and lands in the Gumbel
domain; its marginal can look heavy-tailed by mixture while every
conditional law is light, which would invalidate (C1) silently. The
maximum, not the mean, is the object this framework applies to.

The corpus is a 2025 Common Crawl snapshot (FineWeb), chosen because its
filtering is heuristic: corpora filtered by perplexity or by quality
classifiers truncate the upper tail of $Y$, a selection on the response.
The model is Pythia-410m (fp32 throughout; the reference model of the
likelihood-ratio variant is Pythia-1.4b), whose public training corpus
predates the crawl. The primary analysis scores exactly $T=512$ tokens
per document after a 64-token burn-in, so document length is fixed by
construction; a secondary analysis leaves $T$ free. Covariates are never
computed from the forward pass that produces $Y$: $p=272$ surface and
frozen-encoder-embedding covariates survive a continuity filter, with
$n=20{,}000$ documents, and at the tuning of
Section~\ref{sec:tuning} the regime is $2n\alpha h\approx232$ local
extremes per interior window. The marginal generalized Pareto fit gives $\xi=1.16$ with profile
confidence interval $[1.07,1.25]$, the marginal Hill estimate at the
$10\%$ level is $1.28$ --- the gap between the two reflecting
threshold sensitivity --- and the sixteen stratified fits give
$\xi\in[1.14,1.29]$ with every profile interval excluding $0$: the
conditional positivity that (C1) requires and that a marginal Hill
plot cannot establish. Numerical saturation, degenerate document classes, and
fp32 rounding are ruled out by direct checks in the replication
material.

\subsection{Calibrated screening and a null result}

Ranks alone never refuse to answer: a ranking exists whether or not
any signal does. The screening here is therefore calibrated by
permutation: for $B=1{,}000$ permutations $\sigma$ of the responses
against the covariate matrix, the full screen is rerun and the
familywise threshold is the empirical $5\%$ quantile of the null
minimum score $\min_j\widehat\Psi_j^{(\sigma)}$ (scores rank
small-first). The null hypothesis this tests is joint independence of
$Y$ and the covariates --- permutation destroys scale dependence and
index dependence alike --- so it is a global test, not a test of the
tail-index-specific hypothesis; a covariate could move the scale of
$Y$ and reject under it. On the primary analysis, no covariate of the
$272$ crosses the familywise threshold, for either response variant.
The per-covariate $5\%$ thresholds are exceeded $31$ times for $Y$ and
$23$ for $Y_\Delta$ against $13.6$ expected under independence, an
excess concentrated in the strongly dependent embedding-PCA block and
absorbed by the familywise threshold; we read the outcome as no
familywise-significant covariate, not as a textbook uniform null. The same holds for
$1{,}024$-dimensional blocks of internal model activations (residual-stream
averages over a past prefix, screened against $Y$ computed on the
disjoint future suffix, at $n=50{,}000$): every layer configuration is
at or near chance level. An uncalibrated reading of the raw rankings
would have produced discussable leaders in every one of these
experiments; the calibration is what makes the null legible, and we
regard it as part of the method's proper use.

\subsection{Two scale clusters with known status}

\paragraph{Document length: a natural nuisance, asymptotic truth.}
For weakly dependent regularly varying surprisals,
$\E[\max_{t\le T}S_t\mid x]=\gamma(x)\log T+O(1)$: length enters the
scale of $Y$ and never its index. On nested prefixes of the same
documents --- composition held fixed --- the measured slope of
$\E[\max S]$ against $\log T$ is $1.23$ with intra-document confidence
interval $[1.15,1.31]$, containing the independent Hill estimates of
$\gamma$; across free-length strata the Hill intervals overlap while
the location moves by $2.24$ nats. When $T$ is left free, the screening runs on the $16$ surface
covariates that survive the continuity filter in that mode, five of
which are length proxies (characters, bytes, words, whitespace count,
gzip size). Those five fill the top five positions of the quantile
screen at every level $\tau\in\{.90,.95,.99\}$ --- for one level, the
chance probability that a prespecified five-element block occupies the
top five of sixteen is $1/\binom{16}{5}\approx2.3\times10^{-4}$, and
the three levels are strongly dependent, so we quote no combined
probability --- while the tail-index screen places none of them in its
top five and ranks the block in its bottom quartile (median rank $13$
of $16$ against $3$ for the quantile screen). Under the permutation
calibration of the free-$T$ run, no covariate reaches the familywise
threshold for $Y$, and a single covariate crosses for $Y_\Delta$. This
is the B1 dissociation on real data, with a nuisance an order of
magnitude stronger than the simulated one, visible in the rankings and
absorbed by the familywise test.

The same experiment locates a boundary of validity: at $n=2{,}000$
($2n\alpha h\approx65$) the tail screen itself ranks the length block
first --- the second-order bias of the local Hill statistic responds to
a scale shift of this amplitude --- and the artefact disappears at
$n=20{,}000$ ($2n\alpha h\approx232$). Specificity against scale is an
asymptotic property with a measurable finite-sample onset.

\paragraph{A grafted cluster: exact truth.}
By Breiman's lemma, if $\Pp(Y>y\mid x)$ is regularly varying with
index $-1/\gamma(x)$ and $V>0$ is bounded and independent of $Y$ given
$x$, then
$\Pp(YV>y\mid x)\sim\E\{V^{1/\gamma(x)}\}\,\Pp(Y>y\mid x)$: the index
is unchanged and only the scale constant moves (boundedness makes the
moment finite for every $\gamma$). Grafting
$V=\exp\{\kappa(\Phi(F)-1/2)\}$ with a latent $F\sim N(0,1)$ and
$q_{\rm scale}=20$ observed proxies of $F$ (loading $0.7$, as in B1)
onto the real responses produces twenty covariates that provably carry
no information about the index while shifting every finite quantile,
inside the real correlation structure of the $272$ genuine covariates.
Across the paired strength sweep $\kappa\in\{0,0.5,1,2,4\}$, the
number of proxies among the twenty leading positions grows for the
quantile screen from $1$ ($\kappa=.5$) to $19$ ($\kappa=2$) and $20$
($\kappa=4$, all three levels; median proxy rank $10.5$ of $292$). The
tail-index screen retains $2$ to $4$ (selected pair; median proxy rank
$143$--$175$, at or beyond the chance value of $146$) and $0$ to $5$
(aggregated; median proxy rank $126$--$181$ up to $\kappa=2$, falling
to $66.8$ at $\kappa=4$). The quantile screen's behaviour is coherent
for its own target throughout; the experiment measures an
order-of-magnitude sensitivity gap, not an immunity --- at $\kappa=4$
both variants of the tail screen show visible contamination ($4$--$5$
proxies, one of them first), and minimum-rank aggregation does not
help here and degrades the median proxy rank, since there is no
dispersed tail signal to stabilize and nine passes only pool noise. The two findings must be kept separate: under the permutation
calibration nothing is declared in any arm for any screen --- no proxy
ever reaches the familywise threshold, so neither method makes a false
discovery --- while the target separation of this section lives in the
uncalibrated rankings, which are what a practitioner reads when
choosing $d$ variables to retain. The raw rankings are more
contaminated than the calibrated procedure.

\subsection{What this application does and does not establish}

An external criterion, computed without any screen --- the dispersion of
stratified $\gamma$ estimates per covariate, calibrated against random
strata --- identifies which covariates actually modulate the index in
these data (a single clear case, the whitespace rate, with
non-overlapping quartile intervals). Both screening families rank
$\gamma$-modulating covariates equally well by this criterion, the
quantile screen at $\tau=.95$ marginally better. The evidence of this
section is therefore evidence of \emph{specificity}: covariates that
move finite quantiles without touching the index --- whether by
asymptotic argument (length) or by exact construction (the grafted
cluster) --- capture every fixed-quantile ranking and not the
tail-index ranking. It is not evidence of superior power for finding
index-active covariates, and the simulations of
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} say the same: the advantage of the
tail-index screen is what its leading positions contain, not how often
it finds a shared signal first.
