\title{Tail-Index Sure Independence Screening for\\Ultra-High-Dimensional Heavy-Tailed Regression}
\author{Anonymous manuscript}\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We propose a marginal screen for the covariates that determine a positive
conditional tail index, designed for problems where the dimension $p$ is large
relative to the number of observations informative about the tail. The screen
rests on one geometric fact: after projection onto a single coordinate, the
conditional distribution is a mixture whose tail is governed by its heaviest
component, so the projected tail index is the upper envelope of the tail-index
surface along the corresponding fibre. Inactive coordinates produce flat
envelopes at the global maximum $\gmax$; active coordinates are detectable
exactly when their fibres miss the global argmax set on a set of positive
measure. Ranking coordinates by the average projected index therefore
separates detectable active variables from noise. We estimate the envelope
with empirical-rank local Hill profiles, and prove that the maximal score
error over $p$ coordinates is
$O_{\Pp}(\sqrt{\log(pn)/(n\alpha h)})$ plus a bias term negligible relative to
the population gap, where $\alpha$ is the local tail fraction and $h$ the rank
bandwidth; $n\alpha h$ is the effective local extreme sample size. Sure
screening and exact recovery follow when
$\log(pn)=o(n\alpha h\Delta_{\min}^2)$. The algorithm costs $O(pn\log n)$.
A rank-aggregated version of the screen, taking the minimum rank over a
block of nine neighbouring tunings, stabilizes the ranking against the
choice of the smoothing parameters. Simulations across
$p\in\{500,1000,2000\}$, including a model whose scale is driven by a
correlated cluster of twenty inactive covariates, show when screening on
the tail index differs from screening on a fixed high quantile: the
scale cluster distracts fixed-quantile screens at every level, while the
aggregated tail screen recovers the tail-index-active set reliably. An
application to violent-crime rates in 1{,}993 U.S. communities, with
$p=99$ continuous predictors against an effective tail sample of about
$200$, illustrates the method in its intended regime, and a second
application to the maximal token surprisal of a language model over
20{,}000 web documents, where two scale nuisances have known status
--- document length by an asymptotic argument, a grafted bounded factor
exactly by Breiman's lemma --- observes the same target separation on
real data under a permutation-calibrated protocol. The uniform
projected-quantile conditions are stated directly; deriving them from a fully
primitive growing-dimensional model remains open.
\end{abstract}
