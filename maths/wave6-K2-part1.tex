\begin{abstract}
This document states the theory in the form that survives all of the
adversarial audits. The identifiable population target is the positive-gap
set relative to the support of the observed covariate law, not a structural
active set defined off that support. The stochastic size of an integrated
sliding score is $n\alpha$, whereas $n\alpha h$ controls only local
feasibility, local profile fluctuations, and spatial bias. Honest inference
requires deterministic tail-bias certificates. Under complete-row
replacement, the bounded sliding-rank score remains robust, but its proof is a
rank-edit telescoping proof, not the false assertion that one changed row
alters only $O(nh)$ windows. A fixed-bin annular construction attains the same
minimax rates when its bin boundaries are externally fixed or otherwise
trusted; fixed bins are not necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is retained and what is not}

The main clean estimator is the empirical-rank sliding local-Hill score,
because direct averaging of its signed R\'enyi expansion removes the spurious
factor $h$ without changing the implemented procedure. A disjoint-block
version is retained only for the fixed-coordinate central limit theorem,
where literal independence gives the shortest proof. The main robust
estimator is the winsorized pairwise-spacing score on the same sliding rank
windows, because it preserves marginal-scale invariance and avoids density
assumptions. The annular pair-trimmed fixed-bin score is retained as an
alternative when genuine fixed bins are available and as a direct estimator
of the untransformed gap. No statement below claims that one good tuning
places all signals in the top $s$ positions of a minimum-rank aggregate, that
unrestricted covariate dependence permits recovery of an off-support active
set, that bare regular variation yields computable finite-threshold bias, or
that a growing winsorization cap has a contamination constant independent of
that cap.

\section{Population target on the observed support}

Let $U=(U_1,\ldots,U_p)\in[0,1]^p$ have uniform one-dimensional
marginals, with arbitrary dependence, and let $Y>0$. All objects may form a
triangular array. Put $S_U=\supp(P_U)$ and assume initially only that
$\gamma:S_U\to(0,\infty)$ is measurable and
\begin{equation}
0<\underline\gamma\le \gamma(x)\le\Gamma<\infty,
\qquad x\in S_U. \tag{2.1}
\end{equation}
For a sub-$\sigma$-field $\mathcal G\subseteq\sigma(U)$, write
$T=\gamma(U)$ and $\xi_{\mathcal G}=\esssup(T\mid\mathcal G)$.

\subsection{The weakest mixture condition used by projection}

Assume that, for $P_U$-almost every $x$,
\begin{equation}
\Pp(Y>y\mid U=x)=c(x)y^{-1/\gamma(x)}\{1+r(y,x)\}, \tag{AR.1}
\end{equation}
where $0<c_-\le c(x)\le c_+<\infty$. For every
$\mathcal G$ used below, namely the trivial $\sigma$-field and
$\sigma(U_j)$, assume the averaged relative remainder condition
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathcal G}(y):=
\frac{\E[c(U)y^{-1/T}|r(y,U)|\mid\mathcal G]}
{\E[c(U)y^{-1/T}\mid\mathcal G]}
\longrightarrow0\quad\text{a.s.} \tag{AR.2}
\end{equation}
This is strictly weaker than a uniform bound
$\sup_x|r(y,x)|\to0$ and is exactly what the projection proof uses.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional projection by exponential tilting]
\label{thm:projection}
Under \emph{(AR.1)--(AR.2)}, for every admitted $\mathcal G$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Pp(Y>ty\mid\mathcal G)}{\Pp(Y>y\mid\mathcal G)}
\longrightarrow t^{-1/\xi_{\mathcal G}},
\qquad t>0, \tag{2.2}
\end{equation}
almost surely. In particular, the unconditional tail index is
\begin{equation}
\gU=\esssup\gamma(U). \tag{2.3}
\end{equation}
If $\gamma$ is continuous on the compact support $S_U$, then the essential
supremum in (2.3) equals $\max_{x\in S_U}\gamma(x)$. The tail index of
$Y\mid U_j=u$ is
\begin{equation}
\xi_j(u)=\esssup\{\gamma(U)\mid U_j=u\} \tag{2.4}
\end{equation}
for almost every $u$.
\end{theorem}
